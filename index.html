When considering retaining wall construction in Brisbane, the initial focus should be on two primary inputs: the required height of the wall and any loads present on the ground above it. This approach is practical because a low garden terrace wall, a boundary wall adjacent to a building, and a wall supporting a driveway present distinct challenges and quoting considerations. Before consulting with a contractor, it is advisable to note the wall's height, length, any visible signs of damage such as leaning or cracking, and whether the area behind it includes a driveway, building, pool, fence, or another wall. Drainage is also a critical structural component, not merely an accessory, as systems like agi drain, gravel, and geofabric behind the wall are integral to the overall wall system. When Building Approval is Required in Queensland

The common understanding that a wall over one metre requires council approval is just one of five conditions under Queensland's Building Regulation 2021 and the Queensland Development Code. A retaining wall can avoid the need for building approval only if it meets all of the following criteria. Brisbane City Council outlines similar triggers on its dedicated retaining wall page.

The five triggers are:

  • Total height over 1.0 metre: This measurement includes the visible wall face plus any retained fill or cut above the natural ground level.
  • Any surcharge load, at any height: This includes elements like a driveway, building, pool, or a second wall situated within the zone of influence behind the retaining wall, even if the wall itself is not tall.
  • Proximity within 1.5 metres of a structure: When a wall is close to a house, shed, or another retaining wall, their structural interactions necessitate approval.
  • Part of a pool barrier: If the retaining wall forms part of a pool fence line, it is assessed as an integral component of the barrier system.
  • Wall plus fence 2.0 metres or more: A modest wall topped with a fence can reach this combined height trigger without either element appearing excessively tall.

It is important to distinguish between building approval and licensing. Building approval is determined by these physical site conditions, whereas QBCC licensing is based on the monetary value of the work. Even a low wall that does not require building approval may still necessitate a licensed contractor. When approval is mandated, the wall is typically designed to AS 4678 standards by an RPEQ engineer and certified with a Form 15. Material Selection: Engineering First

Many property owners begin by inquiring about specific materials, but the recommended sequence places material choice after engineering requirements are established. The wall's height, any surcharge loads, and the drainage strategy are the primary determinants of the engineering specifications. The material selection then follows, influenced by the site's characteristics, accessibility, and the available budget. Concrete sleeper walls, featuring steel-reinforced planks slotted between galvanised posts, are commonly used for Brisbane's cut-and-fill blocks and often serve as replacements for older timber walls. Timber sleeper walls remain suitable for lower garden terraces and stepped beds on sloped inner-west Brisbane sites. Core-filled block walls, reinforced with steel, are appropriate for taller engineered retaining structures and can accommodate rendered finishes. For larger, steeper sites and areas prone to erosion, natural sandstone, boulder, and gabion walls offer robust, free-draining solutions. Regardless of the material, effective subsoil drainage is crucial for the long-term stability of any retaining wall, preventing issues like leaning or bulging over time.

Understanding Retaining Wall Quotes

A comprehensive quote for retaining wall construction should provide more than just a price per lineal metre. Industry practice dictates that retaining walls are priced based on their face area - the product of height and length. Consequently, a wall of the same length can represent a significantly different job if its height varies. Therefore, the quoting method should be clearly stated. Key inclusions to verify in a quote include excavation and earthworks, the type of drainage system specified (agi drain, gravel, geofabric), stormwater connection, the type of posts to be used, the specific materials for sleepers or blocks, and whether engineering design and approval handling are included where physical triggers apply. If a contractor proposes repairs for a wall that is leaning, bulging, cracked, or rotted, it is important to ask for evidence supporting the suitability of a repair approach over full replacement. For retaining wall construction in Brisbane, owners should also ascertain what critical information might still be missing from the quote. A contractor who requests details such as the wall's height, length, and the nature of any loads above it is seeking the same fundamental inputs that guide the initial enquiry process.

  1. Measure Your Wall. Record the existing or proposed height and length of the retaining wall to understand its face area.
  2. Identify Loads Above. Note any driveways, buildings, pools, fences, or other structures located behind the retaining line.
  3. Check Approval Triggers. Compare your site conditions against the stated triggers for building approval (height, surcharge, proximity, pool barrier, combined height).
  4. Consider Drainage Needs. Determine the necessary drainage strategy, including subsoil drainage, gravel, and geofabric, based on site conditions and wall type.
  5. Request Detailed Quotes. Ask contractors for quotes that clearly outline pricing based on face area and include all aspects like excavation, drainage, materials, and engineering if required.

Common questions

What is the main factor determining if a retaining wall needs council approval in Brisbane? The primary triggers for council approval in Brisbane include the wall's total height exceeding 1.0 metre, the presence of any surcharge load above the wall, its proximity within 1.5 metres of another structure, if it forms part of a pool barrier, or if the combined height of the wall and any fence reaches 2.0 metres or more.

How are retaining walls priced in Brisbane? Retaining walls are priced based on their face area, which is calculated by multiplying the wall's height by its length. This method accounts for the volume of materials and work involved, unlike a simple lineal metre price.

Should I choose the material for my retaining wall first? No, it is generally recommended to establish the engineering requirements first, based on the wall's height, surcharge loads, and drainage needs. The material selection should then follow, considering the site conditions and budget.
